# Four residual edges in L-meta-17625

Prepared locally for Amritpal-Nijjar on November 26, 2026 UTC. In a least binary AND/OR circuit in top form over a bipartite four-cycle-free graph, suppose there are exactly two dirty AND gates, no centerless top, and exactly two exceptional OR gates. Under these hypotheses the *entire* residual edge set cannot be a four-edge path or a four-edge fork. The ambient graph and circuit can be arbitrarily large. Both the projection from the physical circuit and the finite contradiction are theorems in Lean. The two shapes are the connected possibilities with four edges and matching number two. The elementary classification of four-edge forests is used here to describe scope. Each checked theorem names its precise shape. [Path](src/PathBound4.lean) · [Fork](src/ForkBound4.lean)

## Counting in the physical circuit

* **A dirty singleton needs a disjoint witness.** If a dirty AND fires on the singleton at vertex u, it must fire on a residual edge avoiding u. Otherwise replace the gate by the input u read twice. Monotonicity preserves rejection, retained edges keep their clean paths, and each residual edge keeps an accepting evaluation. The modified circuit computes the same graph with the same size and contains a duplicate wire, contradicting leastness. This applies to any nonempty graph under the least/top-form hypotheses, for any residual size. [Gate replacement](src/ChangeGate.lean) · [Residual witness](src/ResidualShrink.lean)
* **Distinct missed neighbors cost distinct ORs.** Suppose dirty AND h fires on singleton u. For each residual neighbor v of u on whose pair the other dirty AND is off, some exceptional OR strictly after h has a raw input joined to v by a retained edge. Sharing the same OR across two such neighbors would produce a four-cycle. The number of these neighbors is bounded by the number of later exceptional ORs. If a raw-bearing operand of h fires on a pair with neither endpoint raw, another exceptional OR occurs strictly before h. These results impose actual gate order at every residual size. [Physical routes](src/BranchCharge.lean) · [Counting theorem](src/LeafCount.lean)
* **Visibility and raw support.** A three-edge residual path anywhere in the graph forces both exceptional ORs to be first visible at top inputs when there are exactly two exceptions and no centerless top. Under a single visible exception, two distinct branching centers instead force that gate to have exactly one raw input, a retained common neighbor of the centers. Neither center can then be adjacent to a residual leaf of the other. With two exceptions and one visible, at most one physical wire can violate the raw-support condition (at most one vertex, or a retained common neighbor of every vertex). [Visibility](src/VisibleRaw.lean) · [Centers](src/StarCenters.lean) · [Unique exceptional wire](src/HiddenRaw4.lean)
